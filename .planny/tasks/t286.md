## The decision (t266)

## Background

You parked a task and the board asked "Park t42. What should bring it back?
(optional)". Here is exactly what that box is and what it does.

**What the box is.** It appears wherever the board parks something: the
decisions tab's "Park for now", the drawer's `parked` status button, and a
card dropped on the Parked column. Cancel calls off the whole park and
nothing changes. An empty answer parks the task with no note. Any text parks
the task and keeps that text.

**What gets written.** Parking writes two things to the task's own markdown
file, through the same funnel every other change uses:

- `status: parked`, plus one line in the task's history recording who parked
  it and when.
- `parked_until: <your text>` in the frontmatter, when you typed some.

That is all. The text is free prose, not a date. Waking the task — `planny
todo`, the board's "wake" or "bring back" button, or any other status change
— deletes the note, so a stale reason never outlives the parking it explains.
`planny doctor` reports a `parked_until` sitting on a task that is not
parked, and can drop it.

**What reads it.** `planny show` prints a "parked until:" line, the drawer
shows one, and the decisions tab's Parked list shows "until …". An agent
reading the task sees it in the body of `planny show`.

**What nothing does with it.** Nothing acts on the note. No date is parsed,
nothing wakes the task when a condition comes true, no queue watches it. It
is a reminder for whoever reads the task next — you, or an agent.

**Why this is worth deciding.** The question "what should bring it back?"
promises more than the field delivers: it sounds like the tool will bring the
task back. It also interrupts a gesture meant to be quick — "Park for now"
replaced "Skip for now", which was one click. And there is a gap: once a task
is parked, the board offers no way to change or add the note. The only way in
is to park it again.

## Why this comes to you (the operator)

You met the box and could not tell what it was for, which is the clearest
possible evidence about the wording. Whether the note stays, changes its
words, or grows teeth is a product judgement about your own tool, and one of
the options costs real work.

## Proposal

Park in one click, and move the note into the drawer.

Clicking Park parks the task at once and offers "undo", like every other
board action since t258. The note becomes an ordinary field on the parked
task in the drawer — a one-line box labelled "parked until", saved with the
rest of the form — so it can be written after the fact, corrected, or left
empty. Its help text says plainly what it is: "A note for whoever picks this
up. Nothing acts on it."

*Pros:* the quick gesture stays quick; the promise in the wording goes away;
the note becomes editable, which today it is not; nothing is lost, because
the field and everything that reads it stay exactly as they are.
*Cons:* a reason typed later is a reason often not typed at all — the prompt
catches it at the one moment you know why.

## Alternative options

- **Keep the box, fix the words.** Ask "Note for later (optional)" and say
  underneath that nothing acts on it. Cheapest possible change, and it keeps
  the reason captured at the moment you have it. Weaker: it still interrupts
  a one-click gesture, and the note still cannot be edited afterwards.

- **Make the note do something.** Accept a date as well as prose; a parked
  task whose date has passed comes back to `planny next` and the board on its
  own, and the board shows "wakes on 14 Sep". This is what the current
  wording promises. Weaker: it is real work — a date parser, a wake check in
  the queue, a doctor rule for dates in the past — and a task that reappears
  on its own can surprise an agent mid-plan.

- **Drop the note entirely.** Parked becomes a plain status, one click, no
  field. Simplest of all. Weaker: a parked task then says nothing about why,
  and "parked" without a reason is where work goes to be forgotten — the
  problem the whole feature exists to prevent.
