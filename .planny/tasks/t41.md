## Background

Every task carries a field that says who does it: `ai` or `operator`. The
field is named `kind` (frontmatter `kind:`, flag `--kind`). Tasks also
carry `type` (task or decision). "Kind" and "type" are near-synonyms that
name different concepts, so labels need glosses ("kind (owner)") and the
operator has already asked which word is canonical. The founding prompt
offered both names: a task "owner" or task "kind".

## Why this comes to you (the operator)

The frontmatter key is part of the store format you ratified in t1: after
other projects adopt the store, renaming a key costs every adopter a
migration. Today exactly one store exists (this repo's own) and the
package is unpublished, so the rename is at its cheapest. If the answer is
no, everything stays as is and the UI keeps the "kind (owner)" gloss.

## Proposal

Rename `kind` to `owner` everywhere: frontmatter key, `--owner` flags
(keep `--kind` as a hidden alias for one release), UI labels, skill,
README. The parser accepts legacy `kind:` and rewrites it on the next save
of each file; a one-shot sweep migrates this repo's store immediately.
`owner` means the responsible category (ai | operator); the specific
session that claimed a task stays the "holder" (started-by), unchanged.

*Pros:* the field name says what the field means; ends the kind/type
confusion; done before t4 (publish), no outside migrations ever.
*Cons:* touches many files in one sweep; muscle memory and old chat logs
say `--kind` (the alias softens this).

## Alternative options

- `assignee` — reads as a specific person or session, which is the
  holder's job, not this field's. Ruled out for the collision.
- `role` — describes the actor, not the relation to the task; vaguer.
- Keep `kind` — zero cost now, permanent gloss cost later, and the rename
  only gets more expensive after publish.

## Needed from you

"rename" or "keep".

## When

Before t4 (publish). After publish the rename costs every adopter a
migration script.

## Outcome

Keep kind as it is. Operator: renaming to owner invites confusion between owner (the responsible category) and holder (the session that started the task). The kind/owner gloss in labels is the acceptable cost.
