Why: the board can only be driven with a mouse. The only keys that do
anything are Enter in the search box, Enter in the add-task notice, and
Cmd/Ctrl+Enter to save the drawer. Escape closes the search panel but not the
drawer, and the search results cannot be picked with the arrow keys — the
operator must reach for the mouse in the middle of a keyboard action.

Done looks like:
- Escape closes the drawer, then the search panel, in that order.
- "/" focuses the search box from anywhere outside a text field.
- Up and down arrows move through the search results, and Enter opens the
  highlighted one.
- Up and down arrows move between cards in the board and rows in the tree,
  and Enter opens the highlighted task in the drawer.
- A "?" key opens a short list of these keys.
- Every shortcut stays inert while the focus sits in a text box.

Files: web/app.js, web/style.css (a focus ring for the highlighted card),
test/ui.test.ts.


Built, 2026-09-02.

- "/" puts the cursor in the search box, from anywhere outside a form.
- Up and down walk the cards on the board, the rows in the tree, and the
  search results. Enter opens the one the cursor sits on.
- Escape closes the nearest thing first: the key list, then the search
  results, then the drawer.
- "?" shows the key list; its own button or Escape puts it away.
- Every shortcut except Escape stays inert while the focus sits in an input,
  a textarea or a select.

One correction to the plan above: it said Escape closes "the drawer, then the
search panel". The other order is right — the search panel sits over the
board and is the more transient of the two, so it goes first.

The walk moves real focus, not a private cursor, using a roving tab stop:
exactly one card (or row) is tabbable at a time, and the arrows carry that
stop with the focus. So the reader tabs into the list once instead of tabbing
past every card, the browser draws its own focus ring, and a screen reader
follows the walk. That also does part of t257's work.

The search box's own Escape handler is gone: Escape now belongs to the single
document handler, which is the only place that knows what else is open. With
both in play the two fired in turn and one keypress closed the panel and the
drawer together.
